# Magician & Mentalist — 100 Music Cues

100 distinct MP3 recordings, 13 role-based playlists, and an offline Show Control import package. The collection is curated from Kevin MacLeod's official catalog under CC BY 4.0; use the supplied credits for the tracks you perform with.

## Start here

- Open `Library.html` for searchable local playback, waveform previews, track choices, and the effect index.
- Import `Show-Control-Magic-Music-100.zip` through Show Control → More → Settings → Import library backup. It adds a new show with 13 playlists; it does not start audio. Import applies the package's English/Dark Blue preferences. Set your preferred language/theme again afterward if needed.
- The `mp3` folder contains human-readable filenames grouped by role. Copy it wherever you keep your show music. No videos, streaming references, API keys, or AI-generated audio are required.
- The bundle carries the same 100 MP3s, 13 original category covers, credits in every song's notes, independent cue definitions, trims, levels, and fades.

## Playback defaults

Every cue starts at source time zero, plays at 1×, has no countdown, and requires manual advancement. Autoplay, looping, and crossfade are off. Crossfade duration is saved as 2 seconds for a later deliberate enable. Fades are in real time. Each playlist has role-appropriate starting trims; the complete downloaded file remains available for editing. Four very long ambient pieces are supplied as six-minute MP3 excerpts with an ending fade, with changes identified in their credits.

Levels use measured integrated loudness and true peak to apply attenuation only. Speech beds start softer than entrances and releases. These are rehearsal starting points: your microphone, loudspeaker, room, and voice determine the final balance. No track is boosted, and peak headroom is retained.

## Music direction

1. Invite a volunteer in clear speech, then start walk-up music. Fade it as they reach you.
2. Use a bed while an audience task truly needs time: writing, shuffling, sorting, walking, or moving seats. If the task is merely long, shorten its handling as well; music alone will not make it interesting.
3. Establish the effect and its conditions in silence. Do not rhythmically underline hidden handling or make music changes appear to cue a participant.
4. For theatrical apparent danger, build atmosphere with a sparse bed, move to a stronger passage only for the visible escalation, and stop before the decisive moment. No track is a safety timer or a stunt instruction.
5. Let the result be seen, verified, and understood before pressing a release cue. Leave intimate answers and personal names unscored.
6. A repeated musical motif can unify a show. Avoid changing genres for every trick or replacing an earned emotional scene with a generic sentimental cue.
7. Close-up music is optional. At a noisy table, silence often gives you better connection and clearer instructions than another sound layer.
8. For a dedicated interval playlist only, consider outgoing crossfade plus incoming autoplay after rehearsal. The performance playlists stay manual. Do not enable loops until you have chosen and checked the join.

## Effect-by-effect suggestions

Named effects do not establish your exact handling, duration, method, or ownership of a particular artist's act. Rehearse entries and exits against your own version; no borrowed soundtrack or technical method is claimed.

## Three practical scene recipes

**A volunteer arrives:** 010 Easy Lemon → fade to silence for introductions → 070 Floating Cities while a thought is written → fade to silence for the answer → 082 Inspired after recognition → 012 Happy Alley as they return to their seat.

**A playful visual sequence:** 003 Welcome to the Show for entrance → 021 Marty Gots a Plan for changing props → silence for the final object → 081 Take a Chance once it is clear → 091 Arroz Con Pollo for the applause / walk-off. Use the actual track numbers in the browser/catalog as the source of truth.

**A serious suspense scene:** 052 Oppressive Gloom for setup → 061 Hitman for visible escalation → silence for the decision and outcome → 086 Rising Game for a dark theatrical release, or 082 Inspired for relief → 089 Heroic Age for the bow. Do not chain these automatically.

## What was verified

100 MP3 files were downloaded through yt-dlp, inspected by ffprobe, decoded in full with FFmpeg, hashed, and checked for unique payloads. Waveforms and loudness measurements were computed from the audio. The ZIP has 114 stored UTF-8 entries and content-addressed payloads. App-domain validation and native import evidence are recorded separately in `validation-report.json`.

Musical phrase boundaries, subjective fit, venue balance, live PA quality, and physical Android/iOS playback are rehearsal/device checks; a successful decoder test is not evidence that those have been auditioned. No AI service was used.
