## Native and release follow-up remains required

The audit does not accept the native store bridge solely because shared UI guards pass. Android's `pendingPurchasePromise` is assigned only after asynchronous product lookup; raw concurrent bridge calls, queued connection callbacks on teardown, synchronous callback exceptions and main-queue presentation need native regression coverage. The actual pinned Expo component defaults to `Queues.DEFAULT`, and this module declares no main-queue override. [Google's integration contract](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/integrate) requires `launchBillingFlow` on the main thread; source inspection is sufficient to open this issue, not to claim a device reproduction or fix. iOS concurrency/cancellation/presentation must be audited against its actual async module and StoreKit lifetimes as well.

Both platforms also need proof that an idle-started product lookup cannot present a new checkout after a prepared show takes ownership. Merely rechecking JavaScript before entering the bridge does not establish that native callback guarantee. Do not block free preparation on an unavailable store or silently drop completed paid transactions as a workaround. These are the next native-admission/teardown actions, not a reason to claim the customer goal complete or strip the optional store feature.

The resolved DataTransport dependency and observed scheduled service must inform the final SDK privacy/network inventory. No wire payload or user-data disclosure is established by its name alone. Store transaction, refund/revocation, transfer/offline and privacy-policy evidence still requires configured platform test accounts, appropriate builds and devices. No purchase, restore transaction, AI generation or credential read occurs in this pass.
